PURE DRIVING

About VROOM

I built VROOM because I wanted to drive, and everything I opened wanted something else from me first. An account. A season pass. Nine menus and a tutorial before a single corner.

So: you open a page, you pick a circuit, you pick a car, you drive. That is the whole product. There is nothing to buy, nothing to unlock, nothing to subscribe to, and no account. I am not going to add any of that later either, because the moment you add it the game stops being about driving and starts being about the thing you added.

This is a game built for fun. Mine, first. If it is yours too, better.

The 90s are the target

Top Gear. Daytona. Cruis'n USA. What those got right was not the graphics, it was the distance between wanting to drive and driving. You put a coin in and you were at speed in four seconds. Nobody explained the handling model to you. You learned it by hitting a wall.

That is the feel I am after, and it drove real decisions. Flat shading and low poly, because a clean readable road beats a photoreal one you cannot place your car on. Chunky dials you read at a glance. No loading screens worth the name. Choosing a circuit starts the race, there is no press any button to continue in between.

The physics are real, though

Arcade look, honest simulation underneath. This is the part I care most about and the part nobody sees.

The car is a rigid body on a raycast vehicle controller in Rapier, at a fixed 120 Hz step with the render interpolated between. Four independent suspension struts with real spring and damper rates. Load transfer that actually moves grip between the tyres, so braking into a corner unloads the inside rear and you feel it. Tyre grip that falls off with load, which is why weight transfer produces understeer and oversteer instead of just numbers changing.

Engine torque comes from a curve, through a gearbox, through a final drive, to the contact patch. Aero drag and downforce both scale with the square of speed. Surface grip is per wheel, so putting two wheels on the grass costs you exactly what it should.

Every car is specified from the real thing and then verified against the real thing. Not typed into a table and hoped for. The numbers on the car select cards are read out of the running simulation, so a card physically cannot promise something the car will not deliver.

The Lamborghini quotes 770 hp at 8500 rpm and 720 Nm at 6750. Those two only agree if the torque curve keeps pulling well past its peak, so I solved backwards for what it needs at 8500 and built the curve from that. It makes exactly 770. That is the level this is done at.

Feeling the limit

A driving game lives or dies on whether you know the car is about to let go before it lets go. On a real car that comes through the wheel and the seat. Here there is no wheel, so it has to come through your eyes and ears.

  • The tyres start talking before the limit, not after it. Front and rear squeal at different pitches, so you can hear which end went.
  • Skid marks, tyre smoke and the squeal are all driven by one number. They are the same event seen three ways, so they can never disagree.
  • The body leans on its springs further than the physics does, because the outside front tyre is the one about to give up and you need to see the weight arrive on it.
  • The camera leads a slide up to 15 degrees, so when you are sideways you can still see where you are actually going.
  • Engine audio is real recordings, pitched and crossfaded by rpm and load. A V12 fires twice as often as a flat six, so the Lamborghini sits most of a fifth above the Porsche.

No ABS. No traction control. No stability control. These cars are as fast as they are unassisted, and that is deliberate for now.

Six circuits

Each one is a JSON file: a hand laid centreline plus its own palette, fog, sun angle, terrain and surface grip. I shape them by hand in an editor built into the game, because a circuit is only fun if someone moved the corners until it was.

Dirt is my favourite and it is the one that uses the rest of the game hardest. Three quarters of a lap is spent sliding. On tarmac a slide is a mistake; on gravel it is the technique.

What there is to do

Beat your own time. That is it, and for now that is enough. Every circuit keeps your best lap, and the gap between your best and your current one is the whole game. Take all six.

Coming: more cars, more circuits, and multiplayer. Racing someone is the obvious next thing and it is the one I want most. It will arrive when it is good, and it will arrive free, like everything else here.
