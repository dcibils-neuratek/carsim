What this is

Bringing back the game
you could just start

Somewhere between the arcade and now, games got heavy. Megatronik Studio makes the other kind: small, fast, finished games you open and play, built with the feel of a 90s cabinet and the reach of a modern browser.

01

Four seconds to fun

The arcade contract: coin in, and a few seconds later you are already playing. No sign-in, no lobby, no shop standing between you and the first corner.

02

Nothing to buy

No accounts, no subscriptions, no currency, no battle pass, nothing to unlock. The moment a game gets a store, it stops being about the game and starts being about the store.

03

Real physics. Arcade fun.

Anyone should be playing inside ten seconds — and underneath, an honest simulation, so a 300 hp coupé and a hypercar are two genuinely different animals rather than the same one with a bigger number on it.

Arcade driving · Play in browser · Free

Pick a circuit, pick a car, drive. Flat-shaded and readable like the racers it comes from — and underneath, four wheels on four springs, weight that moves when you brake, and tyres that give up gradually instead of all at once. Every car is built from the real one's torque curve, gearing and drag, then measured in the running game to make sure it does what the card says.

It is not trying to be Gran Turismo and it is not trying to be Assetto Corsa. A real car, in an arcade world: six cars across five drivetrain layouts, six circuits, and nothing to set up before you are allowed to move.

Who

One person,
making all of it

Megatronik Studio is one man. Design, code, physics, art, audio, this page. I have been playing games for thirty years — NES, SNES, Dreamcast, PS2, PS3, PSP, PS4, PS5, Xbox, PC, and most of what came in between — and at some point wanting to make one stopped being a someday thing.

The studio is also how I learn. Every game gets me a little further into rendering, physics, feel and the strange craft of making a machine enjoyable to operate. I write down what I figure out and ship the thing either way.

On AI

AI can't make something fun.
That part is still mine.

I use AI heavily — code, assets, all of it. It lets one person build at a scale that used to need a team, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

But fun is not a thing you can prompt for. It comes from playing the build for the hundredth time, moving a number by two hundredths, driving the same corner until it feels right, and watching someone else pick up the controller and seeing the exact moment they get bored. That is the job, and it is still a human doing it.
